## 1. Qualification: is Lotsa a medical device (MDR Art 2(1)) or an accessory (Art 2(2))? MDCG 2019-11, Rule 11, eIFU

### Takeaway
As described (generic, works with any manufacturer's IFU, no patient data, no patient-specific outputs, refuses clinical decisions), Lotsa most likely falls **outside** the MDR. Its purpose is to retrieve and present existing manufacturer information about how to operate equipment. That is not a "medical purpose" for individual patients, and Lotsa is not intended "for use with particular devices" in the sense of an accessory. The weakest point is the accessory/"directly assist" limb, together with the fact that generative AI does more than "simple search." Claims about safety, alarm handling or correct therapy delivery could tip it in.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] MDR Art 2(1): a medical device is any "...software... intended by the manufacturer to be used, alone or in combination, for human beings for one or more of the following specific medical purposes: diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease...". The test turns on the *manufacturer's intended* medical purpose. — [MDR 2017/745, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj)
- [LAW] MDR Art 2(12): "intended purpose" means the use for which a device is intended according to the data supplied by the manufacturer on the label, in the instructions for use or in promotional or sales materials or statements, and as specified by the manufacturer in the clinical evaluation. Marketing therefore counts. — [MDR 2017/745, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj)
- [LAW] MDR Art 2(2): an accessory is "an article which, whilst not being itself a medical device, is intended by its manufacturer to be used together with one or several particular medical device(s) to specifically enable the medical device(s) to be used in accordance with its/their intended purpose(s) or to specifically and directly assist the medical functionality of the medical device(s) in terms of its/their intended purpose(s)". — [MDR 2017/745, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj); summarised the same way in the Commission's MDSW material: [EC MDCG 2019-11 PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en)
- [GUID] The MDSW qualification flowchart in MDCG 2019-11 has an explicit step asking whether the software is an accessory under Art 2(2), before the MDSW assessment. The guidance also notes that a software accessory may be one "driving or influencing the use of a medical device". — [EC MDCG 2019-11 PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en); [EC docsroom](https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/37581/attachments/1/translations/en/renditions/native)
- [COMM] CMS: the "specifically and directly assist" limb is "inherently broader" than "enable" and could bring software within the MDR as an accessory. — [CMS Law](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/digital-health-the-new-regulation-of-medical-software-and-apps)
- [COMM] Johner Institute: software can be an accessory, but if it is used for medical purposes without the hardware, it is itself a medical device. Example: a pulse oximeter extends a monitor's functionality. — [Johner Institute](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/accessories-for-medical-devices-definition-and-regulatory-requirements/)
- [GUID] MDCG 2019-11: software intended only for non-medical purposes (for example word processing, back-up, wellness) is not MDSW. — [EC MDCG 2019-11 PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en)
- [COMM] Summary of MDCG 2019-11: software that only gives health information without reference to an individual is not MDSW. Hospital administration and scheduling software is usually not a device. — [Zechmeister Solutions](https://zechmeister-solutions.com/en/blog/mdcg-software-classification-examples-startups); [mediacc](https://www.mediacc.de/en/medizinprodukte-wiki/mdcg-2019-11-leitfaden-zur-qualifizierung-und-klassifizierung-von-software-unter-mdr-ivdr)
- [GUID] MDCG 2019-11 decision tree (from the guidance text as I know it, not re-read here): step 3 asks whether the software performs an action on data beyond "storage, archival, communication, simple search or lossless compression", and step 4 asks whether the action is "for the benefit of individual patients". Both must be yes for MDSW. — [EC MDCG 2019-11 PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en) (verify the wording)
- [GUID] MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 was published on 17 June 2025. Commentators disagree on how much changed. Emergo/Casus describe a "fairly significant rewrite" with an expanded note on MDSW, and say AI (MDAI) is explicitly covered. OpenRegulatory says the qualification logic and the structure of Rule 11 were not changed. — [EC news 2025-06-17](https://health.ec.europa.eu/latest-updates/update-mdcg-2019-11-rev1-qualification-and-classification-software-regulation-eu-2017745-and-2025-06-17_en); [Casus Consulting](https://casusconsulting.com/mdcg-2019-11-rev-1-updated-guidance-software-qualification-classification-mdr-ivdr/); [Emergo by UL](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/european-revision-primary-software-guidance-mdcg-2019-11-revision-1-small-changes-meaningful); [OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11)
- [LAW] Rule 11 (MDR Annex VIII): software that provides information used to take decisions for diagnostic or therapeutic purposes is class IIa (IIb or III depending on the possible harm). Software that monitors physiological processes is IIa/IIb. "All other software is classified as class I." — [MDR 2017/745, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj); class I as residual category confirmed in [EC MDCG 2019-11 PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en)
- [COMM] For LLMs, "intended purpose" set by the manufacturer is the decisive criterion. DORDA argues that if a system *in practice* influences diagnostic or therapeutic decisions it is MDR-relevant "even if the provider denies such purpose". — [DORDA](https://dorda.at/en/news/regulatory-boundaries-age-health-ai); see also [Cambridge JLME](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-law-medicine-and-ethics/article/walking-backward-to-ensure-risk-management-of-large-language-models-in-medicine/CBBE0ECCA34FCB82432342234D3F52BD)
- [LAW] eIFU, Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/2226: device manufacturers may provide instructions for use electronically, as a rule only for professional users. For software, the IFU may be provided by the software itself. Amended by (EU) 2025/1234, which widens the scope of devices for which eIFU is permitted. — [EUR-Lex 2021/2226](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2021/2226/oj/eng); [Consolidated 2025-07-16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2021/2226/2025-07-16/eng/pdf); [EUR-Lex 2025/1234](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/1234/oj/eng); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2024/09/electronic-instructions-for-use-for-medical-devices)
- [GUID/COMM] Läkemedelsverket has a page "Is it a medical device?" (January 2021). Its content could not be read here. Under Swedish rules, an authority that doubts whether a product is a medical device can refer the question to Läkemedelsverket. — [Läkemedelsverket](https://lakemedelsverket.se/en/medical-devices/manufacture/is-it-a-medical-device); [Förordning 2021:631](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-lagar/dokument/_sfs-2021-631)
- [COMM, closest Swedish precedent] Läkemedelsverket planned inspections of manufacturers of AI documentation assistants ("ambient scribes") in spring 2026. According to an opinion piece in Läkartidningen, the agency judged that an AI documentation assistant needs CE marking as class IIa "because the information affects care decisions". — [Läkemedelsverket press release via TT](https://via.tt.se/pressmeddelande/4272364/lakemedelsverket-granskar-ai-assistenter-i-sjukvarden?lang=sv); [Läkartidningen](https://lakartidningen.se/nyheter/lakemedelsverket-ska-granska-ai-assistenter/); [Läkartidningen opinion](https://lakartidningen.se/opinion/debatt/medicinsk-ai-maste-leva-upp-till-hoga-krav/)

### Inferences
- [INF] **The MDSW route:** RAG with generated answers is probably more than "simple search", so step 3 can be answered yes. The decisive step is 4/medical purpose: answers about how to operate a pump or clear an alarm concern the *device*, not an *individual patient*. That argues for "not MDSW", provided no answers are tailored to a patient (dosing, settings for patient X). A plainly "verbatim extract + page reference" design is easier to defend as "search/communication" than free-form reformulation.
- [INF] **The accessory route:** Art 2(2) requires an intention to be used with "particular" devices to "specifically" enable or "specifically and directly" assist their medical function. A generic tool for any IFU is a weak fit. The risk rises if Lotsa is marketed for, say, "Pump X from manufacturer Y", or with claims that it is needed for correct or safe use. The device's own IFU is already the device manufacturer's labelling (MDR Annex I ch. III, 23). Lotsa does not replace it, and should say so.
- [INF] **Reformulation risk:** If the AI paraphrases IFU content, Lotsa produces "new" instruction text that is no longer the manufacturer's validated labelling. That does not make it a device per se, but it raises the product-liability and patient-safety risk, and the risk that a supervisory authority sees it as "information used for therapeutic decisions" (for example, an infusion pump set-up = therapy delivery). Verbatim citation with forced source display reduces both risks.
- [INF] **If it were judged to be a device:** "how do I set up the pump" for an infusion therapy could be argued to be information used for therapeutic decisions (Rule 11(a), IIa). A pure operating/maintenance help without a therapeutic decision would be class I (the residual category). The Swedish documentation-assistant precedent (IIa) shows that Läkemedelsverket reads "affects care decisions" broadly.

## 2. How intended purpose, labelling, marketing and refusal design determine qualification; claims to avoid

### Takeaway
Qualification follows the manufacturer's *documented* intended purpose, which per Art 2(12) includes labelling, IFU, marketing and statements. A disclaimer does not help if the function or the marketing in practice points to a medical purpose. Intended purpose, marketing, UI and refusal logic must therefore be consistent.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] Intended purpose = the manufacturer's information on the label, in the IFU, in promotional or sales material, statements and the clinical evaluation (Art 2(12)). — [MDR 2017/745, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj)
- [LAW] MDR Art 7: prohibits labelling, IFU and advertising that mislead about the device's intended purpose, safety and performance (applies to devices). — [MDR 2017/745, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj)
- [COMM] "the intended use ... is defined through product information like the label, advertising, and claims". — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12680717)
- [COMM] A disclaimer is not enough if the system in practice influences diagnostic or therapeutic decisions. — [DORDA](https://dorda.at/en/news/regulatory-boundaries-age-health-ai)
- [COMM] Sweden: the organisational setting (healthcare vs. something else) does not decide qualification. Intended purpose does. — summary in search results based on [Förordning 2021:631 / Riksdagen](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-lagar/dokument/_sfs-2021-631) and [Regeringen](https://regeringen.se/contentassets/d79c8e7023fa46fb92388a96cb382b40/lakemedelsverket.pdf) (weak sourcing, check)

### Inferences
- [INF] **Suggested intended purpose (outside the MDR):** "Lotsa is a search and reading tool that helps staff find and quote passages in instructions for use that the organisation itself has uploaded. Lotsa does not replace the instructions for use, does not provide recommendations about examination, treatment, dosing or patient-specific settings, and is not intended to be used to take or support clinical decisions. The instructions for use from the manufacturer of the equipment always apply."
- [INF] **Claims to avoid:** "increases patient safety", "reduces medication errors/incidents", "ensures correct use of the infusion pump", "alarm management/alarm support", "decision support", "replaces the instructions for use/training", "validated against manufacturer X", "for ventilator Y" (risks the accessory route), "helps you set the right flow rate/parameters for the patient", "triage", "monitoring". Even statistics on patient outcomes in sales material can create a medical purpose.
- [INF] **Refusal design as evidence:** Document refusal rules (no patient-specific values, no dosing, no "which setting suits the patient") in requirements and test reports. Log that answers are always verbatim citations with page references. Show the citation more prominently than any summary. When the source is missing, answer "not in the manual, contact MT/the manufacturer". This supports the claim that the *function* matches the non-medical intended purpose, which is the core of the DORDA argument.
- [INF] **Hospital adaptations:** If the customer uses Lotsa as part of its own clinical procedures (for example, linked to a medication process), the customer may itself create a new intended purpose. Contract and instructions should limit use.

### Gaps
- No Swedish or EU decision found that tests a refusal design as a factor in qualification.

## 3. If Lotsa is a device: route to CE marking, requirements, Swedish requirements, cost and timeline

### Takeaway
Class I (Rule 11 residual category): self-declaration without a notified body, but a full MDR system (QMS, technical documentation, clinical evaluation, PMS, registration). Class IIa: notified body (Annex IX QMS + technical documentation sampling), typically 12–24 months and significant cost, plus annual audits. For a small Swedish company, IIa is a major step up.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] Class I: the manufacturer issues an EU declaration of conformity after drawing up technical documentation (Annexes II and III), Art 52(7). Class IIa: Annex IX (QMS + assessment of technical documentation on a representative basis), or Annex XI, with a notified body, Art 52(6). — [MDR 2017/745, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj)
- [LAW] The manufacturer's general obligations in Art 10: risk management, clinical evaluation, technical documentation, QMS (Art 10(9)), PMS (Art 83–86), vigilance (Art 87–92), UDI (Art 27), registration (Art 29–31), a person responsible for regulatory compliance (Art 15; micro and small enterprises may have the person permanently and continuously at their disposal). — [MDR 2017/745, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj)
- [LAW] MDR Annex I 17.2: software must be developed according to the state of the art taking into account the development life cycle, risk management, verification and validation. In practice: harmonised standards EN ISO 13485 (QMS), EN ISO 14971 (risk), EN 62304 (software lifecycle), EN 62366-1 (usability), with IEC 82304-1 for standalone health software as the state of the art. — [MDR 2017/745, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj) (the list of standards is [INF] / general practice, not verified against the current harmonisation decision)
- [LAW, Sweden] Förordning (2021:631): documentation under MDR Art 52(12) is to be in Swedish or a language accepted by the notified body. Läkemedelsverket may issue regulations (for example HSLF-FS 2021:32 on providing information and reporting). — [Förordning 2021:631](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-lagar/dokument/_sfs-2021-631); [SFS2021:631 PDF](https://svenskforfattningssamling.se/sites/default/files/sfs/2021-06/SFS2021-631.pdf)
- [GUID, Sweden] Läkemedelsverket: reporting of FSCA/FSN goes to Läkemedelsverket for the time being, since EUDAMED is not yet fully functional or mandatory. SFS 2025:287 contains transitional provisions linked to the EUDAMED roll-out. — [Läkemedelsverket legislation PMS/vigilance](https://lakemedelsverket.se/en/medical-devices/surveillance-vigilance/legislation-on-post-market-surveillance-and-vigilance); [SFS 2025:287](https://www.svenskforfattningssamling.se/sites/default/files/sfs/2025-04/SFS2025-287.pdf)
- [COMM] Cost: a 2026 budget guide gives class I as about €8,000–26,000 and 3–6 months (likely excluding internal QMS work). The IIa figure could not be read. The notified body fee is usually the largest item. — [MedDeviceGuide](https://meddeviceguide.com/blog/ce-marking-cost-medical-devices-guide) (low reliability)
- [COMM] Notified body waiting time of 12–24 months for initial assessment. Certificates for IIa+ are valid for up to 5 years with annual surveillance audits. — [MedDev Insider (Substack)](https://meddevinsider.substack.com/p/master-eumdr-2017745-conformity-assessment) (low reliability); [Kiwa FAQ](https://www.kiwa.com/en-ca/it/industries/medical/faq-medical-devices/)
- [LAW/COMM] Implementing Regulation 2026/977 (May 2026) standardises notified body procedures, partly to reduce cost uncertainty and delays. — according to search summary, [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/ja/blogs/asprescribed/2026/05/recent-eu-and-uk-medical-devices-developments-eu-mdr-ivdr-eu-ai-act-uk-medical-devices-regime) (verify)
- [COMM] MDR/IVDR revision proposal COM(2025) 1023 (16 Dec 2025) is still in the legislative process (October 2026). The Parliament committee vote is pending, with an indicative plenary on 14 December 2026. — [Sidley, 2026-10-16](https://goodlifesci.sidley.com/2026/10/16/a-more-competitive-medtech-landscape-in-the-eu-what-the-mdr-ivdr-revision-could-mean-for-medtech-manufacturers-and-investors/); [Eversheds](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/united-states/insights/proposed-eu-mdr-ivdr-revision-a-regulatory-system-at-a-crossroads); [Regeringen fakta-PM](https://regeringen.se/contentassets/d79c8e7023fa46fb92388a96cb382b40/kom-forslag-revidering-mdr-och-ivdr.pdf)

### Inferences
- [INF] Realistic for a small Swedish company: class I = a QMS based on ISO 13485 (certification not required for class I), IEC 62304 documentation, risk file, usability file, clinical evaluation (for IFU software, mainly literature and performance validation of citation accuracy), PMS plan, actor/product registration, an internal person for regulatory compliance. Roughly 6–12 months and several hundred thousand SEK to 1–2 MSEK including consultancy, mostly internal time (not sourced, an estimate). IIa = add notified body fees and annual audits, plus 12–24 months.
- [INF] An LLM component makes 62304/14971 work harder (non-determinism, hallucination as a hazard, change control when the model is swapped). Under the MDR, a model swap can be a "significant change" (under IIa requiring the notified body).
- [INF] Swedish-language labelling: Sweden generally requires information to users in Swedish (lag 2021:600 / förordning 2021:631). Not verified in the text here. See Gaps.

## 4. In-house exemption (MDR Art 5(5)) and hospitals' obligations (HSLF-FS 2021:52)

### Takeaway
A region/hospital that builds its own similar tool can use Art 5(5) (only if the tool is a device in the first place, and then with conditions such as no equivalent product on the market, a QMS, a public declaration, IVO supervision in Sweden). Hospitals that buy Lotsa have their own management-system obligations under HSLF-FS 2021:52 for *medical devices*. If Lotsa is not a device, those regulations do not formally apply, but procurement will often ask for equivalent evidence.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] Art 5(5): devices manufactured and used only within health institutions established in the Union are exempt from most MDR requirements (except Annex I GSPR) if conditions are met: not transferred to another legal entity, an appropriate QMS, a justification that the patient group's needs cannot be met by an equivalent device on the market, information to the authority, a public declaration, etc. — [MDR 2017/745, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj)
- [GUID] MDCG 2023-1 is guidance on Art 5(5). The example "a health institution that develops in-house medical device software used on site by its medical staff" falls within scope. — [MDCG 2023-1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-01/mdcg_2023-1_en.pdf); [EAHP summary](https://eahp.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/in-house_guidance_stakeholders.pdf)
- [GUID, Sweden] IVO has forms and information on manufacturing and use of in-house medical devices (reporting of incidents with in-house products). — [IVO info](https://www.ivo.se/globalassets/dokument/publikationer/blanketter/medicintekniska-produkter/info-om-tillverkning-anvandning-egentillverkade-medicintekniska-produkter-211011.pdf); [IVO reporting form](https://www.ivo.se/globalassets/dokument/publikationer/blanketter/medicintekniska-produkter/anmalan-negativa-handelser-tillbud-med-egentillverkade-medicintekniska-produkter-210715.pdf)
- [GUID/COMM] Belgian FAMHP has run a market-surveillance action on in-house software. — [FAMHP](https://www.fagg-afmps.be/sites/default/files/content/POST/MEDDEV/08%20Rapports%20de%20survaillance%20du%20march%C3%A9/Thema-actie_in-house_software_NL.pdf)
- [GUID] MDCG 2025-6 (according to Hogan Lovells): in-house devices are not high-risk AI under Art 6(1) unless they are assessed by a notified body. — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/mdcg-published-new-guidance-on-the-interplay-between-the-mdr-ivdr-and-the-ai-act)
- [LAW, Sweden] HSLF-FS 2021:52 (Socialstyrelsen): "Varje vårdgivare ska fastställa rutiner för användning och hantering av medicintekniska produkter i verksamheten" ("every care provider shall establish routines for the use and handling of medical devices in its operations"). The routines cover user training, following the manufacturer's instructions, and registration in the maintenance system. The regulations define "informationssystem" (information system). — [Socialstyrelsen, consolidated](https://www.socialstyrelsen.se/kunskapsstod-och-regler/regler-och-riktlinjer/foreskrifter-och-allmanna-rad/konsoliderade-foreskrifter/202152-om-anvandning-av-medicintekniska-produkter-i-halso--och-sjukvarden/); [lagen.nu](https://lagen.nu/hslf-fs/2021:52); [PDF](https://www.socialstyrelsen.se/contentassets/0156188a15454314a925ab34909ef324/2021-6-7503.pdf)

### Inferences
- [INF] The fact that HSLF-FS 2021:52 requires staff to follow the *manufacturer's instructions* and to have training is a strong argument for Lotsa's positioning: Lotsa must lead to the manufacturer's text (verbatim citation), not compete with it. Hospitals' MT/MTA units will probably want a risk analysis, validation of citation accuracy, version handling of IFUs (the right IFU version for the right serial number/software version of the equipment), and a log. Offer these as "supplier documentation" even without CE marking.
- [INF] Risk: an outdated IFU in the library is a realistic hazard. Version and update handling should be a built-in function and part of the customer's procedures.
- [INF] The in-house route is not relevant to Lotsa as a commercial supplier. It is a competing alternative for the regions. MDCG 2023-1 requires a justification that the need cannot be met by an equivalent CE-marked product. A CE-marked Lotsa would in theory make that route harder for regions; a non-CE Lotsa would not.

## 5. The EU AI Act (2024/1689): high risk, Art 50, Art 4, GPAI, timelines incl. the Digital Omnibus (2026/1744)

### Takeaway
- If Lotsa is **not a device**: not high-risk via Art 6(1). Annex III does not cover it (point 5(a) concerns public authorities' assessment of eligibility for care services, not technical support). What applies is Art 4 (AI literacy, softened) and Art 50(1) (informing people that they are interacting with AI, unless obvious). Art 50 applies from 2 August 2026.
- If it **is a class I device**: still not high-risk, because Art 6(1)(b) requires third-party conformity assessment.
- If it **is class IIa**: high risk under Art 6(1)/Annex I Section A. After the Digital Omnibus the requirements apply from **2 August 2028**. The pending MDR revision (COM(2025) 1023) proposes moving the MDR to Annex I Section B, which would largely remove the AI Act high-risk requirements. Not adopted as of November 2026, and the sources are partly contradictory.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] AI Act Art 6(1): an AI system is high-risk if (a) it is a safety component of a product, or itself a product, covered by Annex I legislation and (b) that product must undergo third-party conformity assessment under that legislation. The MDR is in Annex I Section A. — [AI Act 2024/1689, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- [GUID] MDCG 2025-6 (19 June 2025): an MDAI is high-risk under Art 6(1) if it requires notified body assessment under the MDR/IVDR. The MDR class determines the outcome. ### Inferences
- [INF] Roles: the Lotsa company = **provider** of an AI system (Art 3(3)) that integrates a GPAI model (local open-weight model). It is not a GPAI provider as long as it does not fine-tune with more than a third of the training compute. Hospital = **deployer** (Art 3(4)). GPAI obligations (Art 53) sit with the model supplier (for example Meta/Mistral/Alibaba). Lotsa should keep documentation from the model provider (Art 53(1)(b) information) to support its own risk analysis.
- [INF] Art 50(1) for Lotsa: show clearly in the UI that answers are generated by AI (cheap, and supports the positioning). Art 50(2) (machine-readable marking of synthetic text) probably has little practical weight for an internal Q&A tool, but the exceptions (for example "assistive function for standard editing" or that it does not substantially alter input) should be assessed. Verbatim citations support that exception.
- [INF] Art 4: both the company (provider) and the hospital (deployer) are covered, in a softened form after 2026/1744. Offer training material and a "AI limitations" section.
- [INF] Annex III: no point seems to apply. Point 5(a) concerns public authorities' assessment of entitlement to health services, and point 2 critical infrastructure concerns safety components in the management of digital infrastructure, traffic, water, gas, heating, electricity. A hospital information tool does not fall there. — check [AI Act Annex III, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- [INF] Strategic consequence: staying outside the MDR, or at class I, avoids the AI Act high-risk regime entirely. Class IIa would bring a double regime from August 2028 (unless the MDR revision changes it).

### Gaps
- The OJ text of 2026/1744 has not been read. The Annex I date and the Art 50(2) grace period come from secondary sources.
- The Gleiss Lutz vs. Sidley conflict about the Section B move is unresolved. Check the Council/Parliament legislative observatory (procedure for COM(2025) 1023).
- Whether Art 4 in its amended form applies from 27 July 2026 or another date (sources are vague).

## 6. Swedish market surveillance authorities (the AI Act and the MDR)

### Takeaway
MDR: Läkemedelsverket is the competent authority and market surveillance authority for manufacturers/products. IVO supervises use in care and in-house manufacture. AI Act: the government decided in June 2026 on interim assignments (until 31 December 2026). PTS coordinates and is the single point of contact. Läkemedelsverket covers high-risk AI under Art 6(1) in medical devices/IVD. IMY covers certain areas, among them law enforcement. The supplementary Swedish law (SOU 2025:101) does not appear to be adopted.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW/GUID] Government assignment: Läkemedelsverket is to be market surveillance authority for "AI-system med hög risk som avses i artikel 6.1 ... relaterade till produkter som omfattas av" ("high-risk AI systems referred to in Article 6.1 ... related to products covered by") the MDR and IVDR, and notifying authority for conformity assessment bodies. - [INF] For Lotsa as a non-device: the Art 50 supervisor is probably PTS (residual/coordinating authority) in the interim. For Lotsa as a device: Läkemedelsverket for both the MDR and (at IIa) the AI Act.
- [INF] Läkemedelsverket's active interest in AI assistants means a documented qualification analysis (a "qualification memo") should be prepared and kept ready. A voluntary dialogue/borderline question to Läkemedelsverket may be worth considering before launch.
